1. One joke, four instruments, one number

If a measurement is real it should not care which machine takes it. The same reference joke was measured on the pinned Kaggle run, an in-kernel transformers Gemma, a local llama.cpp build, and a requantisation at four times the precision.

2. The predeclared follow-up, executed

Our own ablation blamed a bad setup/punchline split for a null result on headline edits. So we re-split every item at the edited word, against the old splitter and a placebo cut. If the split was the problem, the correlation should appear.

share of items where resolution registered at all

Verdict: anchoring the split at the joke's actual seam roughly doubles how often the mechanism engages, and the placebo moves nothing — so the lift is real. But it predicted funniness no better. The format boundary is not a splitting artifact. That is a sharper falsification than the hypothesis it was built to test.

3. Where the instrument breaks, stated plainly

Native setup/punchline jokes, no splitter involved, genuine pairs against pairs whose punchline was swapped in from a different joke. The frames here were written by the model itself.

genuine pairs shuffled pairs

Verdict: with model-written frames the instrument does not merely fail, it inverts. Asked to explain a punchline that does not belong to its setup, the model builds an elaborate bridge that collapses surprisal harder than an honest frame does, and a fixed decoy control cannot absorb a per-item confabulation.

The clearest single case: the setup “A doctor tells a woman she can no longer touch anything alcoholic” was given the foreign punchline “If you beat your fish, it dies!”. The model's frame states outright that the punchline is unrelated, and the pair still measured R = 1.426, ten times the mean genuine pair.

Why this strengthens the system: resolution is trustworthy exactly when the frame's provenance is. Our acceptance gate already refused model-invented frames; it now rests on a measured population rather than a single crafted probe. This does not touch the certified regime, which uses given frames and still separates three jokes from two controls on both quantisations.

4. Do cheap features beat the expensive instrument?

Thirty-four deterministic surface features — syllable counts, word lengths, rarity against our own corpus, alliteration, rhyme — computed in microseconds, versus signals that cost seconds per item. Correlations against human funniness grades.

surface feature Gemma signal

Verdict: on raw correlation, cheap features outrank the expensive ones. But testing thirty-four features against one outcome and reporting the winner is how a noise ranking gets published, so we applied Benjamini-Hochberg control. Nothing survives — cheap or expensive. At this sample size neither surface structure nor teacher-forced surprisal reliably predicts these human grades, and the apparent leaderboard is noise wearing a ranking.

5. Is EmbeddingGemma actually the right choice?

The retrieval claims rest on one embedding model. We ran the same three fixed probes through every backend installed locally, so the choice is checkable rather than assumed.

Verdict, split three ways and reported as such: the headline cross-lingual retrieval is a three-way tie, so that result is a property of the dual-channel design rather than of EmbeddingGemma. EmbeddingGemma clearly wins paraphrase semantics. nomic wins frame-family retrieval, which is an open lever we have not taken. Collapsing three probes into one ranking would only have hidden which probe the weighting favoured.

What this project is claiming

A joke is a controlled prediction error with a cheap, audience-permitted repair. That claim is turned into four measurable signals: surprise, resolution net of a decoy null, efficiency per frame token, and a separately-gated persona judgment. The apparatus is Gemma end to end — Gemma 2 as the certified instrument, Gemma 4 generating and judging, EmbeddingGemma for retrieval, Gemma 3 in the frame-writer leaderboard.

What makes it a measurement rather than a rating is that it is allowed to fail, and it has, three times: a confabulation failure fixed by a decoy null, a leak failure fixed by an overlap guard, and the frame-provenance inversion above. Each failure is receipted in the repository next to the result it undermines.
